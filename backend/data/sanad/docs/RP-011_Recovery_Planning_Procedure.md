# RP-011 Recovery Planning Procedure

## 1. When a recovery plan is needed

A recovery plan is prepared when an engine is at risk and the cause is on its critical path. The plan sets out the options, the completion each would give, the cost and who must approve it.

## 2. Options for a late part

Three options are evaluated for a critical part that will arrive late.

Alternate stock. Use a unit held in another store. The unit must have its configuration confirmed before issue, and the transfer is approved by inventory control with configuration confirmation.

Supplier expedite. Pay the supplier or carrier to deliver earlier. The spend is approved by Procurement with logistics confirmation and recorded against the work order.

Re-sequencing. Do in advance the preparatory work that the approved maintenance instructions permit, so that less remains to do after the part arrives. This is authorised by the supervisor and has no direct cost.

<!-- page 2 -->
## 3. Evaluating the options

Each option is scheduled forward to the completion it would give, on the same basis as the forecast. Options are compared on completion, cost and the approval they need. Options can be combined: re-sequencing can run alongside either of the other two.

## 4. Approval and tracking

An option is in force only when its approval is recorded. Until then the forecast does not change. Once approved, the purchase order or stock record is updated and the forecast is recalculated.

## 5. Reassessment

The plan is reassessed whenever a new dependency appears, for example when a finding disposition adds repair work or material. The reassessment checks the approved actions against spares, personnel, inspection slots, inventory, labour and workstation availability, and states whether the plan still holds and how much buffer remains.
